# From a drawing brief to an editable poster or infographic

[Return to the skill](../SKILL.md)

## Write the brief an illustrator could act on

Name the reader, where they will see the piece, the question, the supported answer and what they should do next. Specify the smallest display or print viewing distance, the actual copy and its longest translation. Choose the typeface and explain its fit before composing. Use the engine's approved font policy. Start the page on light paper with dark ink. Assign the accent to the action or object. Pair a status colour with a word or distinct shape.

Write one sentence with an actor, verb, object and visible consequence. Replace “an efficient archive” with “the archivist lifts the folder flap so the visitor can see the attached slip”. Pick two observed setting details that help explain that action. If the logo were hidden, what would still identify this workplace? If a prop earns its place only by filling an empty corner, remove it.

For a series, record the recurring person's build, age, skin tone, hair silhouette, clothing and working accessory. Prepare a front view, a three-quarter view and two task poses. Attach cleared references with a role beside each one: cast, prop, site or composition. Use sketches or sanitised material when the real transaction contains private details. Keep documentary evidence and conceptual illustrations visibly distinct.

## Review the work as the client's audience will see it

Use the same craft standard for a consulting report, a software onboarding screen and a public campaign. Name one observed detail the reader would recognise, one repeatable drawing decision the client can own, and one layout decision that makes the message easier to use. For a lending service that might be the worn cloth spine, the clipped corner repeated in the illustrations, and a caption aligned with the return slot. Choose fresh details for the next client.

Lay the art into the real deliverable before accepting it. On an app screen, preserve the control's prominence. On a strategy exhibit, give the decision and its evidence the strongest reading order. On a poster, stand back until fine lines disappear. If replacing the art with another client's stock image leaves the meaning intact, rework the staging or remove the image. An immaculate vector can show as much authorship as a carved print. Sprinkling grain, wobble or decorative marks over a generic scene adds no craft.

## Generate the artwork

1. Choose a direction in the [drawing recipes](drawing-recipes.md) and state the reason in terms of the reader's task. Sketch two thumbnails under the same headline. Mark the focal edge and the exact clear area needed for type, data and crop.
2. Use the selected **Draw** paragraph as the starting prompt. Change its scene to the commission. Include the medium, silhouette, inside and outside edges, light or shadow, camera, palette roles and area reserved for type. Replace all bracketed fields before sending a brief.
3. If the user requested artwork, use the available drawing or image tool within the brief. For a brief-only request, deliver the filled prompt. With no renderer, deliver the prompt and layout specification and record that the image and proof are unassessed.
4. Compare variations at intended size. For a style comparison, hold actor, action, prop, copy and crop fixed. Make each candidate in its own render before assembling the comparison sheet. Generating several different cartoon treatments in one image can give every panel the same faces, ink weight and paper finish. For a repair, lock what reads correctly and change the smallest failing detail. Retain the prompt, reference IDs, selected artwork and tool settings available.

**Prompt frame**

> Draw [actor] as they [visible action] with [specific prop] at [observed setting]. Show [point of contact] from [view]. Build the subject from [shapes] using [outside line, inner edge, material and shadow]. Use [ground and ink] with [accent] on [focal object]. Keep [cast or prop features] from the attached reference. Reserve [area in this crop] for [specific live content]. Deliver unlettered art with blank screens and tags. The designer supplies the approved mark and information layer.

**Repair example.** A thumb covers the clipped corner that distinguishes a return card from a door pass. Keep the camera, clothes and palette. Move the pinch to the uncut edge and expose both sides of the notch. At final size, check that the notch remains visible against the box. Tweaking saturation will not reveal a hidden corner.

## Build the information layer

| Reader's question | Editable construction | Verify |
|---|---|---|
| How was a total allocated? | Aligned rows with unit, parts, total and any remainder | Recompute from the supplied figures. Name the period and distinguish movement from balance. |
| Which group has more? | Directly labelled bars in one unit on a common zero baseline | Check comparable groups and period against the source table. |
| What changed over time? | Ordered dates and a labelled measure on a line chart | Show gaps and definition changes explicitly. |
| Who acts next? | Role beside each step, labelled conditions on actual branches | Follow every branch to its next owner or unresolved state. |
| Where is it? | Horizontal labels and editable leaders on a checked view | Trace each leader to the correct feature. Label illustrative layouts. |
| Which state applies? | Labelled alternatives with distinct shapes or words | Cover the colour cue and identify each state. |

Keep the illustration out of the plotting area. Draw the chart, arrows and leaders from the approved data or workflow. Put the illustration, information graphic, live copy, supplied logo and background on separate named layers. Record sources, dates, units, calculations and owner with the editable master. An actual interface detail needs an approved, sanitised capture or checked reconstruction.

**Worked assembly.** A fictional branch has 12 returned books. Nine are ready to shelve and three need repair. For a staff infographic, use the flat vector trolley vignette in a narrow column and typeset “Where do the 12 returns go?” above two aligned rows. The copy tells staff to take the three damaged books to the repair desk. Check that 9 + 3 = 12, and mark the figures as training data. The blank spine carries no numbers. On a phone, stack the two rows directly after the question. Alt text can say “Of 12 returns, nine go to shelving and three go to the repair desk.” For a poster about the single return action, use the woodcut card-and-box vignette, one headline and the actual return instruction instead of the table.

## Inspect the delivered size

- Cover the captions. Identify the actor, action and object. Check the working hand, contact point, eyes, shadow direction, object count and continuity. Find a concrete detail that came from the brief. Reject pseudo-text, copied characters and props that imply an unsupported function.
- Read the exact headline, caption and action. Cut any line that merely praises the organisation or describes the drawing twice. Keep claims a subject owner can verify. Use the local infographic or publication gates for the whole piece.
- Recompose each aspect ratio. Check the smallest screen, a compressed copy for messaging, greyscale, and the final-size print on the intended stock when applicable. Measure contrast on the actual background. Protect label–value pairs and the point of contact. Supply a text equivalent with the key action and exact data.
- Open the master, change a caption and export a second size. Retain the version, owner, rights record, references, data sheet, prompt, editable master and chosen exports together.
- Show the export for five seconds to a reader outside the design team. Ask what happened, who acts next and which object or figure mattered. Record their words, the location of any defect, the revision and the result of the repeat. Record a missing proof or reader check as `NOT_ASSESSED`.
