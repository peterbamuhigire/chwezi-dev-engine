# Choose a drawing by what the reader must see

[Return to the skill](../SKILL.md)

Use the reader's question to choose one treatment. Each **Draw** paragraph is an original test brief that can go straight to an illustrator or image tool. Substitute the actual cast, prop, palette and reserved space for the commission. The corresponding **Proof and repair** is an acceptance test for the result. The page number identifies the direction in Chwezi Core Systems, *Illustration & cartoon styles*, Team Edition, November 2026, Field Guide 01. The training scenes below are fictional.

| Reader needs to see | Choose between |
|---|---|
| An exchange between people | Everyday two-person sitcom for a question, civic workplace cartoon for shared responsibility, hand-drawn character storytelling for trust |
| Which action changed something | Newspaper service strip for a handover across time, manga training panels for the precise gesture, mid-century workshop cartoon for the weight or grip of an object |
| How a system works | Retro-futurist systems illustration for relationships, offbeat technical explainer for a single fault, isometric low-poly scenes for physical positions |
| What to recognise or record | Ink and watercolour field guide for a diagnostic feature, clay object study for a tool's profile, layered cut paper for custody |
| What must survive reproduction | Woodcut field poster for a one-ink notice, pop-art editorial spotlight for a single reminder, flat vector editorial for a family of localised assets |

The six entertainment names below are lookup aliases. Use the craft instructions in the production prompt. Design original faces, silhouettes, clothing, tools and signage. If a real product, site or person must be identified, supply the approved capture, drawing or photograph. A recognisable cartoon establishes mood and action. It cannot verify a fact.

## Direct an exchange

### Everyday two-person sitcom · Family Guy · p. 8

**Draw.** At a neighbourhood lending desk, a member turns a blank reservation slip so the librarian can inspect it too. Frame exactly two adults at counter height, in three-quarter profile. Draw rounded shoulders, ordinary adult proportions and even medium ink. Use muted flat clothes against warm white. Give the slip the only strong edge between the two gazes. Keep the top quarter open for two live lines of dialogue.

**Proof and repair.** Cover the dialogue. Both people should be able to see the same face of the slip. If the scene reads as ridicule, level their eyelines, relax the mouth and turn the paper towards them both. Keep the camera and counter fixed for a reply.

### Civic workplace cartoon · The Simpsons · p. 11

**Draw.** Widen the view of a repair desk. A customer watches the intake clerk pass a tagged bag to the repair lead. Compose three distinct profiles at two depths. Carry a steady dark outline around even the smallest figure, fill the clothes with flat local colour and repeat a single apron detail to identify staff. Show the receiving hand open beside the bag. Use a pale wall and reserve a lower band for roles.

**Proof and repair.** A viewer should identify who owns the next action and where the customer stands. If the third person is just background or everyone looks like the same character, shift the receiving hand into clear space and redraw profiles from the cast sheet. Retain natural skin tones and original workwear.

### Hand-drawn character storytelling · Disney, classic 2D · p. 10

**Draw.** An adult visitor pauses at an archive desk holding a closed envelope. The archivist leans forward to listen, palms at rest. Bring both adults to eye level with equal space. Carry a single flowing line from shoulder to wrist, taper the ink on the cheek and use one cel shadow beneath a sleeve. Behind them, dissolve the desk into a warm ivory wash. Leave the upper left empty for a caption.

**Proof and repair.** At thumbnail size the listening posture should survive without a grin or clasped hands. If either adult reads as a child or a mascot, restore adult head proportions and let the tilt of the shoulders carry the emotion. Keep the envelope closed.

## Teach a physical action

### Newspaper service strip · p. 17

**Draw.** Two equal frames of a librarian and a volunteer beside a returns trolley. In the first, the librarian holds out a cloth-bound book, spine towards the volunteer. In the second, the volunteer settles that book upright on the trolley. Lock the counter edge, camera height, sleeves and distinctive cloth spine. Use quick economical black ink on cream, simple eyes and one rust spot on the travelling book. Reserve a caption strip under each frame.

**Proof and repair.** Cover the captions and follow the spine through the gutter. If it changes size, colour or owner prematurely, reuse its outline and redraw the receiving grip. Stack the same frames in reading order for a phone, each with its own caption.

### Manga training panels · p. 12

**Draw.** Open with a waist-up librarian holding a book jacket. Cut to a larger insert from the librarian's viewpoint as a thumb lifts the flap to reveal the empty card pocket beneath it. Thicken just the near edge of the flap. Use crisp black ink on white, broad white gutters and one sparse screentone shadow under the thumb. Keep the pocket face clear for a typeset example.

**Proof and repair.** The close-up should settle which edge lifts. If speed lines or dots obscure it, clear that area and enlarge the hand–flap contact. Check dot interference at phone size and add editable sequence numbers in the audience's reading order.

### Mid-century workshop cartoon · The Flintstones · p. 6

**Draw.** Show the full stance of a standing adult bookbinder seating folded sheets against the wooden stop of a bench jig. Plant both block-shaped shoes on the floor. Build the torso as one rounded wedge, wider at the shoulders, with one legible elbow bend. Use broad uninterrupted flat fills. Make the outside ink visibly heavier than the fine apron seams and taper it at the wrist. Separate the gripping fingers from the paper edge. Put ochre on the board, dark teal on the stop and pale sand behind the bench. Stage the entire action on the right two-thirds. Leave the left third clear for instructions.

**Proof and repair.** Trace the weight from shoulder through palm to the squared sheets. If the hand hovers, move the palm into contact and expose the edge it aligns. Remove period costume or stone-age scenery. Keep the contemporary jig and the drawing's weight.

## Explain systems and space

### Retro-futurist systems illustration · The Jetsons · p. 7

**Draw.** Introduce a conceptual link between a self-check station and a staff desk. Stage slim upright figures beside two widely spaced capsule-shaped consoles on slender supports. Draw smooth ellipses at one level, fine continuous ink and a heavier near rim on the active station. Use vanilla and pale blue with one accent on that rim. Keep both screens blank and the corridor between stations clear for an editable connection.

**Proof and repair.** Trace each official connection from its start to its endpoint after layout. If a decorative orbit implies another transfer, remove the orbit. Check the direction and any offline or queued state with the system owner before drawing arrows.

### Offbeat technical explainer · Rick and Morty · p. 9

**Draw.** An adult technician leans over a label printer and examines a single unplugged connector beside its matching socket. Use slightly awkward angular elbows, a searching tilt of the head and wiry irregular ink. Give one corner of the casing an eccentric curve, with a citrus fill at the connector collar on pale mint. Frame the end and socket unobstructed in profile. Leave the left margin open for a verified status note.

**Proof and repair.** Count the cable ends and identify the visible gap. A light or invented cable can imply a diagnosis. Remove that detail, keep the peculiar casing and give the real state its own live label. Compare any functional port with the equipment reference.

### Isometric low-poly scenes · p. 16

**Draw.** Build an open-sided model of an illustrative reading room with a return box beside the entrance and a sorting table at the far wall. Hold parallel receding edges and true verticals. Give each solid one top value and two distinct side values. Ground the wheels of a small trolley and leave its aisle clear. Put the accent only on the sorting table. Reserve the outer page plane for horizontal labels and leaders.

**Proof and repair.** Follow the aisle without moving through furniture, then extend two receding edges to check parallelism. If a doorway drifts or a leader ends on the floor, rebuild from the footprint. Mark a concept as illustrative. Actual wayfinding needs the checked site plan.

## Identify an object or its owner

### Ink and watercolour field guide · p. 14

**Draw.** Using the supplied leaf specimen reference, show a field recorder holding the leaf by its stalk beside a blank notebook. Draw the identifying margin and vein junction in precise dark pen. Break the contour on the distant leaves and lay a transparent wash inside the subject. Use untouched paper for daylight, muted green and earth for depth. Keep the right margin dry for the observation fields.

**Proof and repair.** Match the diagnostic junction and silhouette to the reference, including in greyscale. If a wash hides the edge or the leaf looks like another species, redraw the contour before repainting. A knowledgeable reviewer checks any identification claim. Enter the record, date and location in live type.

### Clay object study · p. 18

**Draw.** Make a matte stop-motion study of a handheld date stamp, based on the supplied tool profile. Form a rounded grip above a distinct rectangular base. Show the full silhouette and the gap under the grip in three-quarter view. Use a few thumb impressions, a broad light from upper left, a short contact shadow and an ivory tabletop. Keep the stamp face plain and the left half clear for the inventory name.

**Proof and repair.** Compare the height of the grip, base and opening against the reference. If gloss or an extra button makes the tool look electronic, restore the matte surface and remove the feature. Use a verified insert for exact controls or an actual imprint.

### Layered cut paper · p. 15

**Draw.** A cream envelope sits inside one of two open document wallets on a pale paper tabletop. Use two or three cut planes with visibly different edge profiles. Keep the envelope's upper edge rough and the wallet lips smooth. Open up the gap between wallets, use teal and ochre stock and make each short shadow fall down-right. Leave a plain band above both wallets for editable owner labels.

**Proof and repair.** Point to the wallet that physically contains the envelope. If overlap suggests two owners, separate the wallets and tuck the envelope behind only one lip. Use another frame for a later state and put verified connectors on a separate layer.

## Reproduce a message across channels

### Woodcut field poster · p. 19

**Draw.** A librarian's hand slips a blank clipped-corner return card into a wooden box. Frame the wrist to the right with the slot and contact point near centre. Cut the hand and box from one forest-green ink mass on ivory. Use broad white gouges that turn with the knuckles and run with the wood grain. Keep a clean gap around the card's working edge and the top quarter open for a live headline.

**Proof and repair.** Reduce to one ink and proof at the intended distance on the target stock. If wood grain reads as a second slot, remove cuts parallel to the real opening near the card. If a shape cue matters, expose the clipped corner beyond the fingers. Use an optional second ink only after the one-ink silhouette works.

### Pop-art editorial spotlight · p. 20

**Draw.** Crop to the same hand, card and slot for a one-action return reminder. Keep the wrist entering from the right, the insertion edge intact and the upper quarter clear. Use a heavy near-black contour, two broad colour planes and a terracotta accent on the card. Restrict coarse halftone to a small area behind the wrist. Render the card blank. Let the diagonal carry the emphasis.

**Proof and repair.** In a compressed phone preview the card should win attention over the box. If the box is equally bright, mute its fill and reduce the dot field. Remove alarm bursts. Put any comparison or exact figure in the editable information layer or on the next card.

### Flat vector editorial · p. 13

**Draw.** Build a repeatable lending-desk vignette from rectangles, circles and tapered wedges with one consistent corner cut. A clerk places a book in a low trolley. Keep adult proportions, the elbow joint and the cloth spine distinct. Separate adjacent shapes by lightness and reserve the richest of two brand fills for the spine. Leave a plain column on the left for translated instructions. Reuse the same pieces for the next service card.

**Proof and repair.** At small size the book must remain separate from shirt and trolley. If any illustrated office could replace this one, restore the observed trolley handle or book pocket and remove floating decoration. For a scalable deliverable, construct editable paths and check the exported vector. Flat-looking pixels still have a fixed resolution.
