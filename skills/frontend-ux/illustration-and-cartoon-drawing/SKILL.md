# Illustration and cartoon drawing

## Use When

A reader needs to recognise a tool, follow a handover, see who acts next or understand a decision. Use this skill for an original drawn image, an illustrator's brief, or a series of related posters and infographics.

Draw a woodcut for a printed notice, a manga close-up for a training panel or a cartoon for an explanatory poster. Choose the medium by the detail the reader must see.

## Do Not Use When

For a documentary claim about a real site, result or person, use the verified evidence and a factual caption. When an exact interface matters, use an approved, sanitised capture. Keep charts, process logic, figures and captions editable.

## Required Inputs

Establish the audience, action, format, client identity, copy and factual sources. Identify which features of the person, equipment or place must match a cleared reference. Label proposed settings and sample data as illustrative.

## Workflow

1. Use the selection table in [drawing recipes](references/drawing-recipes.md). Name one treatment and why it makes this task clearer. The fifteen recipes include original prompts, construction decisions, proof tests and local repairs.
2. Follow [drawing production](references/drawing-production.md) from two thumbnails to a filled prompt, selected artwork and editable layout. Use the named references only to find a recipe. Direct the tool with the actual shape, ink, colour, camera and gesture.
3. If the commission asks for an image, render the drawing with the available tool. Review it in the intended product or document, correct the visible defect and inspect the export. A request for a brief ends with the usable prompt and layout instructions.

## Quality Standards

Hide the logo and captions. Can a viewer tell what is happening from the posture, point of contact and working object? Inspect the smallest rendition. Simplify a background that competes with the action. Redraw a broken grip, changing prop, invented control or illegible shape. Inspect live copy, data and labels separately from the art.

## Anti-Patterns

A smiling person gesturing at empty space gives the reader no action to follow. Move the working hand onto the object. If the same scene could advertise another client, use a detail observed in this workplace and revise the composition around it. A cartoon reference belongs in the lookup table. Recognisable characters, costumes and logos belong to their owners.

## Outputs

Return the selected art or the requested brief, chosen treatment and reason, references, production prompt and layout instructions. For an infographic or poster include the editable master, the reviewed renditions and a text equivalent. Record the exact defect and correction when a proof is revised.

## Decision Rules

Use one drawing language across a campaign. A wide sequence teaches a handover. A change of camera distance can explain a precise movement. If the evidence is a number, process branch or real location, the verified information layer carries the claim.

## Capability and permission contract

Use the supplied material within its rights and the scope of the commission. Keep personal data out of references. Publication, commissioning and purchases remain with the authorised owner.

## Degraded Mode

If the image tool or a required reference is unavailable, supply a drawable brief and identify the missing proof. Record unrun checks as `NOT_ASSESSED`. Never describe a prompt as a reviewed drawing.

## Evidence Produced

Keep the chosen prompt and image, the source of factual detail, the target-size proof, the review result and the master version together.
